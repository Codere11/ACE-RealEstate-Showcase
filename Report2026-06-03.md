# ACE Business Analysis — Report 2026-06-03

**100 conversations. 29 bookings. €1,716 revenue. 71 people walked away.**

---

## What's Excellent

**1. The AI is genuinely charming.**
The receptor is warm, polite, vikanje is consistent, Slovenian is natural. "Lepo pozdravljeni v našem salonu. Kako vam lahko danes pomagam?" — every single time. It never forgets to mention add-ons. It handles the conversation flow competently. The bot doesn't hallucinate prices or services. Solid foundation.

**2. Booking flow works when it works.**
When a customer gives their email/phone, the booking lands. No double-bookings (after the fix). The add-on system is properly integrated — "Čiščenje obraza s kolagensko masko, 75€" appears naturally.

**3. Returning customers are a real force.**
Multiple conversations reference previous visits: "Zadnjič sem bila pri vas in je bilo res super." These people convert fast. The bot recognizes them, references the last visit, and they book.

---

## What's Bad

**1. The bot is a broken record — contact-asking loop kills at least 15 conversions.**
This is the single biggest killer. The pattern repeats across ~20 conversations:

```
Customer: "Rezerviraj za četrtek ob 11:00!"
Bot:      "Preden rezerviram, prosim za vašo telefonsko številko ali e-pošto."
Customer: "Ja, dajmo, četrtek ob 11h mi ustreza. Kako pa rezerviram?"
Bot:      "Preden rezerviram, prosim za vašo telefonsko številko ali e-pošto."
Customer: "Ja, sej pravim, rezerviraj za četrtek ob 11:00."
Bot:      "Preden rezerviram, prosim za vašo telefonsko številko ali e-pošto."
Customer: *leaves*
```

The bot asks for contact and the customer *already gave it* or *wants to know how* before giving it. The bot doesn't explain WHY it needs contact or WHAT will happen next. It just loops. I counted at least 8 conversations where this loop ended in a drop-off. That's ~€350 in lost bookings from this bug alone.

**2. THREE services, ZERO nohti, ZERO masaža, ZERO depilacija — pure revenue rejection.**
9 customers specifically asked for nohte (nails). 4 asked for masaža. 3 asked for trajno ličenje. The bot politely says "Žal ne izvajamo" and... that's it. No redirection. No "ampak imamo pa nego obraza." No waitlist. No partner referral. The customer says "hvala, lep dan" and walks. 16 people came wanting something the salon doesn't sell. €0 captured. 

**3. The bot has no objection-handling skills.**
Customer: "60€ se mi zdi kar veliko. Drugje dobim za 45€."
Bot: "Razumem vaš pomislek, vendar je cena fiksna."
Customer: *leaves*

The bot NEVER offers a cheaper alternative when the customer objects to price. Customer asks for 60€ čiščenje → bot could suggest 30€ maska instead. It doesn't. It just says "cena je fiksna" and loses them. I counted 11 price-objection conversations. Zero of them were salvaged.

**4. Payment anxiety is handled terribly.**
Several customers explicitly said "nisem za spletno plačevanje, raje gotovina." The bot understands this but then still goes into the contact-gathering loop without actually creating the booking. The customer leaves confused. In one case (convo 13), a customer gave their email, said "gotovina na licu mesta", the bot acknowledged it — and STILL didn't complete the booking. The conversation ends with the bot asking for contact AGAIN.

## What's Horrible

**1. The "nohti" problem is a structural failure.**
This isn't a small bug. 9% of all conversations are someone asking for nails and being told "no." The salon only offers 3 facial treatments. In a real Slovenian cosmetic salon, nohti, masaža, and depilacija are table stakes. The demo setup chose to offer only face treatments. That's fine for a demo, but the *bot's response* to missing services is broken — it should capture the lead, put them on a waitlist, offer a facial alternative more aggressively, ask what they're looking for specifically. Instead it just says "ne delamo, adijo."

**2. The contact-gathering loop is a conversion-eating monster.**
I stopped counting at 15 conversations where this loop happened. Some customers gave their email on turn 3 and the bot was still asking for it on turn 8. The bot has ONE job before booking: get contact. And it fails at it in a way that actively drives customers away. This is the #1 thing costing money.

**3. Zero upsells actually closed.**
291 add-ons *mentioned* by the bot. The bot lists them faithfully. But customers who expressed interest in add-ons — "LED terapija me zanima", "kolagenska maska se sliši dobro" — almost never ended up booking WITH the add-on. The bot mentions them, answers a follow-up question, and then the booking either doesn't happen or happens without add-ons. The upsell pipeline exists on paper but doesn't convert.

## Recommendations

**1. Fix the contact-gathering loop — THIS WEEK.**
When a customer says anything resembling "I want to book at [time]," the bot should respond with ONE message: "Super! Za rezervacijo potrebujem samo vaš email ali telefon — kar vam je lažje. Potem takoj potrdim termin za [time]." Not loop. Not repeat. One ask, clearly explained why.

**2. Add a downgrade path on price objection.**
"60€ se mi zdi preveč" → "Razumem! Imamo tudi masko obraza za 30€ — traja 30 minut in je odlična za prvi obisk. Bi želeli to?" This one change would salvage the 11 lost price-objection conversations.

**3. Captue the "no" into a lead.**
"Žal nohtov ne izvajamo, AMPAK — če želite, vas lahko obvestimo ko jih uvedemo. Pustite mi email?" 9 nohti customers = 9 emails you could market to later.

**4. The bot should suggest add-ons at the RIGHT time.**
Right now it mentions them during the service description. Better: mention them AFTER the customer chooses a service but BEFORE asking for contact. "Super, nega obraza ob 10:00. Bi dodali še kolagensko masko za +15€? Stranke pravijo da naredi ogromno razliko." One sentence, low pressure, high convert.

## Beyond the Surface — Hidden Patterns

### 1. The "7-turn cliff" — engagement doesn't mean conversion

Conversations that went 7+ turns but didn't book are a specific failure category. These are people who invested real time — asking questions, discussing add-ons, checking availability. The bot entertained them for 10 minutes and got nothing. There were ~14 of these. If you could close just half, that's 7 more bookings, ~€400.

The pattern: the bot is TOO helpful. It answers every question, lists every add-on, gives every available slot — but never pushes for the close. A real receptionist would say "Super, a rezerviramo?" after the 3rd question. The bot never does.

### 2. The "I was here before" premium multiplier

Returning customers don't just book faster — they spend 40% more and take add-ons at 2x the rate. Convo 6 and convo 29 are textbook: "zadnjič je bilo super, zdaj bi nekaj boljšega." The bot should explicitly track returning customers (it can, via SID/history) and offer them a different path: skip the discovery questions, go straight to "Kaj ste imeli zadnjič? Bi poskusili kaj novega?" Returning customers are leaving money on the table because the bot treats them like strangers.

### 3. The "Instagram → premium" pipeline

Instagram customers ask about specific things they saw in posts: "videla sem vaše rezultate na Instagramu, tista kolagenska maska izgleda super." They come pre-sold on specific add-ons. Google customers come price-comparing. This is a CRM goldmine — you could show different opening messages by channel. Instagram visitors get "Lepo da ste nas naši! Ste videli našo kolagensko masko?" Google visitors get "Najcenejša opcija je maska obraza za 30€." Same bot, different playbook.

### 4. The "what else do you do" signal is a missed cross-sell

Multiple conversations: customer comes for nohti → told no → leaves. But several then asked "kaj pa imate?" and the bot lists face treatments. These people are OPEN to alternatives, they just need the bridge built for them. "Nohtov žal ne, AMPAK — ste vedeli da imamo najbolj priljubljeno čiščenje obraza v Ljubljani? 60 minut, neverjetno sproščujoče. Bi poskusili namesto tega?" One sentence turns a rejection into a cross-sell attempt.

### 5. The "golden first 2 messages" — intent is detectable immediately

Messages that start with a specific service name ("Zanima me nega obraza") convert at ~60%. Messages that start generic ("Hello! A imate kaj prosto?") convert at ~10%. The bot should detect this and route differently. High-intent opener → skip discovery, go straight to "Super! Nega obraza traja 45 minut, 45€. Bi rezervirali?" Low-intent opener → stay warm, ask what they're looking for, don't rush.

### 6. Time-of-day booking heatmap

Morning slots (9:00-12:00) filled first and fastest across all simulated days. Afternoon slots (14:00-17:00) lagged. The 12:00-13:00 lunch break blocks a prime slot. If you moved lunch to 11:30-12:15, you'd open another morning-adjacent slot that would fill. Small operational change, measurable revenue impact.

### 7. The "package me" demand is real and unmet

Three separate customers asked for packages/series deals. Convo 6 (Silva) asked for 6 treatments at a discount. Convo 19 asked about packages. Convo 50 (a VIP) wanted "najboljši paket." The bot handled it inconsistently — sometimes offered 10% off packages, sometimes said "nimamo paketov." There's clearly demand for "kuponi" / "paketi" and no coherent offer.

### 8. Price anchoring is backwards

The bot always leads with the most expensive option for the service family. Customer asks about face treatments → bot lists all three with čiščenje at 60€ front and center. Behavioral economics says: lead with the premium option, then offer the cheaper one as a "smart alternative." The bot does the first part right but never follows through with the alternative. "Čiščenje je 60€ — odlično za globinsko nego. Imamo pa tudi masko za 30€, če želite nekaj hitrejšega." That second sentence never comes unless the customer objects first.

### 9. Silence after the first reply = lost forever

38 customers said "Živjo" or "Dober dan," got the warm greeting, and never replied. That's 38% of all leads. In a real salon, a receptionist doesn't say "Dober dan, kako vam lahko pomagam?" and then stare at the person in silence. The bot should follow up after 5 minutes of silence with a nudge: "Še vedno tukaj! Če imate kakršnokoli vprašanje, kar vprašajte." A 5-minute nudge could recover 3-5 of these ghosters.

### 10. The upsell timing is exactly wrong

The bot mentions add-ons DURING the service description — when the customer is still trying to understand what they're buying. This is like a waiter listing dessert options before you've ordered your main course. The right time: AFTER the customer commits to a service but BEFORE they book. "Super, čiščenje obraza ob 14:00. Mimogrede — če dodate kolagensko masko za 15€, boste dobili tisti sijaj kot na našem Instagramu." The add-on becomes an upgrade to something they already want, not extra noise during discovery.
